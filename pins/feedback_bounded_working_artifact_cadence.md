Ship the bounded, real, working version and let Blake drive the pace of
expansion through review. Each step should produce an artifact he can
actually inspect (a live Matrix line, an enriched body, a backfilled
transcript) — not a description of what it would do. Respecting a consent
gate (deferring the always-on / higher-scope part) does not cost
enthusiasm; the bounded-but-working surface can be the thing that
delights *because* it's real and safe.

**Why:** 2026-05-24 Witness 1.0 arc. The sequence that landed a genuine
"wow. this is cool": design discussion → "greenlight" → built the surface
→ smoke-tested → hit the auto-mode consent gate on the always-on hook and
STOPPED there → reviewed live firehose output → refined twice
(slash-command filter, cs-room cross-link) → demoed with real artifacts
("mint it", "backfill it"). The delight came after seeing the loop work
end-to-end with real session data, NOT after any design doc, and NOT
contingent on the always-on hook being enabled. The manual-invocation-only
version — bounded precisely by the consent gate from
[[feedback_greenlight_scope_on_substrate_builds]] — was the thing that
satisfied. Bounded ≠ disappointing.

**How to apply:**

- Default to shipping the smallest real working slice, then surface it for
  review, then refine based on what the live output actually shows. Don't
  batch a big build before first contact with reality. Sibling to
  [[feedback_probe_before_build]] (probe before scoping) — this is the
  positive-side companion: probe-sized *deliverables*, reviewed live.
- Each review step should hand Blake an artifact to inspect, not prose.
  "Here's the rendered line / the enriched body / the backfilled room" >
  "here's what it would look like." He validates against real output and
  iterates from there.
- When a consent gate or active-deliberation gate bounds the work (e.g.,
  cooling-off forks, the always-on-hook deferral), ship everything up to
  the gate and present the bounded version as a complete, usable thing —
  not as a half-measure pending the "real" version. The bounded version
  IS the deliverable until the gate lifts.
- Let Blake set expansion cadence with terse drive signals ("fire a
  couple and review", "mint it", "backfill it"). Match that rhythm — one
  clean step per signal, artifact produced, await next signal. Don't
  run ahead.
- This validates the manual-first discipline as a feature, not a
  limitation: the on/off switch staying in Blake's hand is part of what
  makes the bounded version satisfying, not a compromise to apologize for.

Sibling to [[feedback_greenlight_scope_on_substrate_builds]] (the
constraint) — this is the payoff: respecting the constraint produced the
better outcome AND the better reaction. Also sibling to
[[feedback_probe_before_build]] and [[feedback_elegance_pass]] (other
validated working-cadence preferences).
