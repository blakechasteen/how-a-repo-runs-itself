**The rule.** A new session / bot / subagent is justified when EITHER (a) its writes are independent of every concurrent participant's writes, OR (b) the work genuinely exceeds a single context window. Absent both, the cheaper-and-better move is to **deepen an existing session**, not spawn another.

**Why.** The mid-2026 agent-harness SOTA (`docs/agent_harnesses_sota_2026-06.md`) has one load-bearing finding that cuts against cheap parallelism: **compute confounding.** Under *equal* thinking-token budgets, single-agent systems consistently match or outperform multi-agent systems on multi-hop reasoning while using far fewer tokens (Tran & Kiela, arXiv 2604.02460) — grounded in the Data Processing Inequality (every agent handoff can only *lose* information, never add it). Anthropic's own data says token usage explains **~80%** of its multi-agent research win, and multi-agent runs cost ~15× the tokens of a chat. So most "multi-agent beats single-agent" results are really "**spent more compute**," not "better architecture." The robust multi-agent wins are narrow: breadth-first, parallelizable, write-independent, high-value work — which is exactly condition (a)/(b).

This bites *us* specifically because our coordination substrate makes spinning up another participant unusually cheap (worktree-per-session, the handoff archive, WSH briefs, `coord.py` locks, the Para swarm). Cheap fan-out is precisely what tempts a system across the line into "just more compute." The architectural bias points the wrong way on the single most important finding in the research, so it needs an explicit counter-stance.

**The single-thread-writes connection.** Condition (a) is the field's #1 multi-agent rule (Cognition: "writes stay single-threaded; additional agents contribute intelligence rather than actions"; Anthropic avoids shared-state peer-to-peer). Parallel writers make conflicting *implicit* decisions and corrupt each other — which is why we derived worktree isolation the hard way ([[feedback_check_main_tip_before_building]]). Fan-out across *reads* (advice, search, verification) is cheap and safe; fan-out across *writes* to the same artifacts is the anti-pattern.

**The carve-out — uncertain claims still want decorrelation.** This pin does NOT say "never run parallel sessions on the same area." For an *uncertain empirical* claim, an independent blind run is **evidence, not waste**: its product is decorrelated corroboration ([[feedback_verify_across_instances]], [[feedback_check_main_tip_before_building]]'s dedup-mechanical/converge-uncertain cut), which equal-compute single-agent reasoning cannot manufacture. So the compute-confounding objection applies to fanning out for *more throughput on settled work*; it does NOT apply to fanning out for *independent judgment on unsettled work*. Keep the two motives distinct.

**The second carve-out (AMENDED 2026-07-06, sid8 e9001764) — wage arbitrage on mechanically checkable work.** The equal-budget question above silently assumes homogeneous token prices. When worker-tokens are much cheaper than orchestrator-tokens AND every task carries an executed check (exit-0 verification — a mechanical-class Proof Block), fan-out pays as cheap parallel labor under an expensive planner/reviewer: premium budget scales with *decisions made*, not lines written. This does not contradict compute-confounding — the win is **economic** (cost per verified artifact), not architectural (no reasoning gain is claimed), and it collapses without mechanical checkability: unverifiable workers lie about "done" ([[feedback_no_fabricated_results]], [[feedback_stub_green_proves_wiring_not_execution]]), and judgment-class review re-centralizes the cost into the expensive model ([[project_judgment_verify_needs_identity_keys]]). Decorrelated external datapoint: Nate B. Jones's Ringer (github.com/NateBJones-Projects/ringer, seen 2026-07-06) converged on exactly this shape with zero contact with this canon — "your expensive model plans and reviews; cheap workers do the typing," executed checks as the only accepted evidence, retry-once-with-failure-context, per-task git worktrees. Same design-space forcing that independently reproduced our collision classes. Boundary conditions, all three required: (a) tasks mechanically checkable, (b) a real wage gradient, (c) writes still independent — condition (a) of the main rule is NOT waived.

**Source anchor + reversal arc (AMENDED 2026-07-22, sid8 d09601c9) — the Cognition citation, made checkable and double-count guarded.** Condition (a)'s Cognition quote is from Walden Yan, *"Multi-Agents: What's Actually Working"* (cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working, published 2026-04-22) — the public reversal, ten months after *"Don't Build Multi-Agents"* (2025-06), by the field's strongest multi-agent skeptic, conceding exactly one legal class: agents contribute intelligence while writes stay single-threaded. **Double-count guard: this is NOT a Ringer-class decorrelated datapoint for the RULE.** The 2026-06 SOTA sweep (`docs/agent_harnesses_sota_2026-06.md`, primary-source list) already ingested this post, and condition (a) descends from it — citing it again as independent convergence on the rule would be circular. (A 2026-07-22 web sweep initially made exactly that error; caught by re-reading this pin + the SOTA doc before amending.) What the post DOES add, one-sidedly decorrelated — their derivation, production failure at Devin scale, has zero contact with this canon: their three working patterns land on this pin's exception structure. Review-agent-with-clean-context (their claim: ~2 bugs/PR caught, 58% severe; unverified here) ≈ the decorrelation carve-out (derived here from [[feedback_verify_across_instances]]); smart-friend capability router + manager-child delegation ≈ the wage-arbitrage carve-out (derived here from Ringer + token-price reasoning); map-reduce-and-manage ≈ condition (b). And "unstructured swarms are mostly a distraction" corroborates the compute-confounding stance from the deployment side. **Practitioner corroboration of the carve-out structure, not independent rediscovery of the rule.** (Folded 2026-07-22, Blake-directed, session d09601c9 graph-engineering web sweep.)

**How to apply.** Before spawning a session / subagent / Para bot, ask the equal-budget question: *would one deeper session under the same token budget do as well or better?*
- **Writes overlap a concurrent participant** (same files / same pins / same corpus region) → do NOT fan; serialize through a lock or fold into one session. This is the collision case worktrees were built for.
- **Write-independent** (separate vertical / separate corpus / disjoint file-set) → fan out; this is real architectural parallelism.
- **Exceeds one context** (corpus too big for a window, map-reduce over N slices like Pinsmith's 6) → fan out; orchestrator-worker / map-reduce-and-manage is the right shape.
- **Same question, just "think harder"** → deepen the existing session instead; another agent here buys compute and loses information at the handoff.
- **Verifying an uncertain claim** → fan out a *sealed, decorrelated* run (don't read their result first — that re-correlates you); the deliverable is convergence-or-divergence.
- **Mechanically checkable + steep wage gradient** (worker-tokens ≪ orchestrator-tokens, every task exit-0-checked) → fan out as cheap labor under an expensive reviewer; without the executed check this collapses into trusting liars, and without the gradient it's just the compute confound again.

Stance, not machinery — the spawn-discipline analog of HBP's emission-discipline ([[project_heavy_bobbin_protocol]]); ship the smallest real unit and deepen before you widen ([[feedback_bounded_working_artifact_cadence]]).

**Tensions-with.** [[project_cross_session_coordination]] frames parallel Claude sessions as a *workforce* property (coordination substrate, not just memory) and rightly celebrates cheap fan-out; this pin is its counterweight — cheap is not free of the compute-confound, and the workforce earns its keep only on write-independent or context-exceeding work, not as a generic "more agents = better." The two are a productive pair, not a contradiction: one says *you can* coordinate many sessions, this one says *when it actually pays*. Relevant to the human-over-fleet design of [[project_anvil_team_coordination_layer]] — Anvil should make the write-independence of a proposed unit *visible* so the fan-out/deepen call is informed, not reflexive.
